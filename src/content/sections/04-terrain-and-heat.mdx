---
title: "Adjusting for Terrain and Heat"
order: 4
summary: "The two variables that make your goal pace a lie."
---

Two conditions reliably invalidate a pace plan built on flat, cool training runs.

### Hills

Climb by effort, descend by rhythm. A useful heuristic: you lose roughly 12–15
seconds per mile for every 1% of sustained grade, and you get back only about half
of it on the corresponding descent. Plan for the net loss rather than pretending
the downhills will bail you out.

### Heat

Above roughly 15°C (60°F), performance degrades measurably, and the penalty
compounds with distance:

| Temperature | Marathon penalty |
| --- | --- |
| 15°C / 60°F | ~1% |
| 21°C / 70°F | ~3% |
| 27°C / 80°F | ~6–8% |

Humidity makes each row worse, because evaporative cooling is what actually moves
heat off your skin. When the air is already saturated, sweat runs off you doing no
work at all.

Adjust the target *before* the start, not at mile 18 when your body has already
made the decision for you.
