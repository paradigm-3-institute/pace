---
title: "The Case for the Negative Split"
order: 3
summary: "Why the second half should be the faster half — and by how much."
---

A negative split means running the second half faster than the first. Nearly every
world record at 5,000m and above has been set this way, and it is not a
coincidence.

Going out fast recruits fast-twitch fibres early, raises blood lactate before your
clearance systems have warmed up, and elevates core temperature ahead of schedule.
None of that is free. Going out one percent slow costs you a handful of seconds you
can almost always take back later.

The practical target is modest:

- **5K–10K:** even splits, or one to two seconds per kilometre faster on the back half.
- **Half marathon:** 15–30 seconds faster over the second half.
- **Marathon:** even effort, which on a flat course means near-even splits and on a
  hilly one means wildly uneven pace.

Note the last point. **Effort is the thing you hold constant, not pace.** On a
rolling course a metronomic split chart will destroy you on every climb.
