---
title: "Executing When It Hurts"
order: 5
summary: "What to actually do in the last third, when the plan meets reality."
---

Everything above is arithmetic. The last third is not.

**Shorten the horizon.** At the point where the full remaining distance feels
impossible, stop thinking about it. Run to the next aid station, the next
kilometre marker, the next corner. The distance does not change; the size of the
problem does.

**Check form, not pace.** When fatigue arrives, cadence drops and stride length
collapses. Cue yourself in order: tall posture, relaxed hands, quick feet. Form
correction buys back more time than willpower does.

**Accept the small loss.** If you are ten seconds per kilometre off target with 8km
to go, you are running a slightly slower race than you planned. That is all. The
runners who detonate are the ones who try to reclaim the whole deficit in a single
kilometre.

---

Pacing is not a constraint on ambition. It is the mechanism by which ambition
survives contact with the distance. Pick the number honestly, hold it patiently,
and let the finish take care of itself.
